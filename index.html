APEX: The Agentic-Physical Experimentation System

Mixed-reality goggles, agentic AI, and human researchers united in a closed loop — bringing real-time reasoning, guidance, and protocol co-development into the microelectromechanical systems (MEMS) cleanroom.

About APEX

APEX is a physical AI framework in which agentic AI provides contextual reasoning, adaptive planning, and real-time analysis, while humans perform dexterous physical execution. Wearable mixed-reality hardware streams experimental context to the AI and delivers in-situ guidance back to the operator.

APEX operates as two interacting real-time loops. Click any step to learn more.

Real-Time Protocol Co-Development Loop

Improve the fabrication recipe

  1. The researcher states the fabrication objective through a chat interface — target device, materials, and constraints — optionally attaching literature, notes, or images of the experimental setup.

  2. The Planning agent composes standard operating procedures from its expert-curated SOP atlas, prior fabrication experience, and targeted literature search — reasoning explicitly over material compatibility and available equipment.

  3. The human stays in control: the researcher approves the proposed protocol, edits individual steps, or sends feedback through multiple conversation rounds — each one logged for traceability.

  4. Real observations flow in from the cleanroom — measured film thicknesses, wafer defects, unexpected failures — grounding the protocol in what actually happened rather than what was planned.

  5. APEX revises the remaining procedure in response to results — tuning parameters, reordering steps, or proposing literature-backed alternative methods when a step proves incompatible.

↻ Repeat and improve in real time

Real-Time MEMS Device Co-Fabrication Loop

Execute the fabrication with real-time support

  1. The human provides what robots cannot: long-horizon execution and delicate motor control — handling wafers, chemicals, and equipment through each step of the procedure.

  2. Mixed-reality goggles stream 8K egocentric video at 32 ms latency, along with hand and eye-gaze tracking and a SLAM-built 3D map of the cleanroom, so APEX sees exactly what the operator is doing and where.

  3. The multi-agent framework aligns each frame to its SOP step, inspects the wafer in real time, watches for unsafe chemical handling, and flags deviations — like a wrong parameter — the moment they occur.

  4. Feedback appears as adaptive 3D overlays in the operator's view: next-step instructions, error corrections, safety alerts, and recovery plans — delivered without interrupting the work.

  5. With the step verified and logged, the researcher moves on — and every action, reading, and correction accumulates into a structured, queryable record of the whole experiment.
